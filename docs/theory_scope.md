Scope and Limitations

1. Scope of the Study

This research is strictly a Spatial Analysis of urban inequality. It isolates the physical and infrastructural dimensions of deprivation to measure "Opportunity" as a function of geography.

    Spatial Domain: The study is bounded by the Vadodara Urban Agglomeration, specifically the 19 Administrative Wards within a 9km radial buffer from the city center.

    Dimensional Scope: The Urban Opportunity Index (UOI) is constructed from three primary pillars:

        Medical Accessibility (Public Hospitals & UHCs).

        Educational Accessibility (Higher Secondary Schools).

        Environmental Vulnerability (Flood Risk & Density).

    Methodological Scope: The analysis is quantitative, utilizing Graph Theory (Network Analysis) and Spatial Econometrics (Moran’s I) to derive statistical proofs of segregation.

2. Limitations (The "Boundary of Claims")

While rigorous, this study operates under specific constraints acknowledged to ensure analytical integrity:

    Infrastructural vs. Social Reality: The UOI measures potential access (is there a hospital nearby?), not realized access (can the patient afford it?). Social determinants such as income, caste, and quality of service (e.g., doctor availability) are beyond the scope of this geospatial model.

    The "Static Network" Assumption: Travel time calculations assume a static road network. While a "traffic friction factor" was applied, real-time temporal variations (e.g., monsoon waterlogging blocking specific roads) are not dynamically modeled.

    Data Granularity: In the absence of micro-level census block data, the Ward is used as the atomic unit. This may result in the Modifiable Areal Unit Problem (MAUP), where pockets of high deprivation within a "wealthy" ward may be statistically smoothed out.

    Causality vs. Association: The study proves a strong negative correlation (r<0) between flood risk and opportunity. However, it claims spatial association, not direct causality. It identifies where the trap exists, not necessarily the historical policy decisions that created it.

<div class="note"> <strong>Defensive Note:</strong> These limitations define the specific lens of the thesis: it is a diagnostic tool for <em>spatial planning</em>, not a comprehensive sociological audit. </div>
