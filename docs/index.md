## Overview

This project models **urban inequality as a spatial and network-driven phenomenon**
using Vadodara city as a case study.

Unlike conventional city-level indicators, the analysis focuses on **accessibility**
— how easily residents can reach essential services using real road networks.

The work forms part of a **Master’s Thesis** submitted to the  
**Department of Statistics, The Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda**.

---

## Research Questions

- Is access to essential services spatially equitable across wards?
- Do deprived areas form persistent spatial clusters?
- How does network-based accessibility differ from distance-based measures?
- Can a data-driven composite index capture multi-dimensional opportunity?

---

## Methodological Pipeline

1. Digitization of post-2011 ward boundaries  
2. Road network extraction from OpenStreetMap  
3. Network-based travel time computation  
4. Construction of an Urban Opportunity Index (PCA-based)  
5. Spatial validation using Moran’s I and LISA  

---

## Key Findings (Phase 1)

- Inequality exhibits **non-random spatial structure**
- Western wards form opportunity clusters
- Central wards show persistent deprivation traps
- Road connectivity is a major equity determinant
